How it works

The questions we get most, answered straight. The full methodology, with every number and the code to reproduce it, is on GitHub.

What's a true level?

Every shooter has a real make rate we can't see directly. We only see his makes, and they bounce around. His true level is our best estimate of the real rate: his last three seasons and this one, recent games counting more, pulled toward the league average until he's taken enough shots to trust. For threes that takes a while; for free throws, about 20 shots.

So he's due?

No. Nobody is owed buckets. A cold shooter's next three goes in at his true level, however cold he's been. When we say "about 20 points a game at his true shooting", we mean: if he keeps getting the same shots and minutes, and they go in at his level, that's what he'd score.

What's a Robbery?

We replay every three-pointer and free throw at each shooter's true level, keep everything else as it happened, and work out exactly how often each team would have won. If the winner would have won less than 35% of the time, it's a Robbery. 35 to 65% is a Coin flip; 65% or more, they Earned it.

Why only threes and free throws?

We tested twos too. How well a team finishes inside turned out to be mostly skill that public data can't separate from luck: counting twos as luck made the chances badly wrong. Threes and free throws are where shooting luck really lives.

Do your numbers come true?

We checked them against seven past seasons and one we kept aside. When we say a team had a 20% chance, it happens about 20% of the time. When we say a player is shooting below his level, his shooting improves by about the amount we said, and our 80% ranges hold about 80% of what happens. Big bounce-back calls go the right way about three times in four.

Can you see how open the shots were?

No. Public data doesn't track defenders. If a player's shots get harder, with a new team or a bigger role, we'll call it luck when it isn't. And minutes and role move scoring more than shooting does, which is why every projection says "same shots and minutes".

Then why don't you rank teams by luck?

We tried. Over months, a team's "shooting luck" is partly real shot quality we can't see, and it didn't predict future results better than plain point differential. So the Teams page shows what happened, not who's better than their record.

Where's the data from?

ESPN's public play-by-play: every shot, its shooter and whether it went in, checked against ESPN's box score each night. The site updates itself three times a night and re-reads the last three days for stat corrections.

## What 90s SLAM actually looked like

From the first 60 covers (1994–2002) and the Fonts In Use credits:

1. **The photo did the shouting.** One player, full-bleed or cut out, usually against a flat
   colour or studio white. The design energy came from photography and type scale, not from
   decoration. There's no tape, torn paper, stickers or rubber stamps on these covers.
2. **The logo is huge and the player overlaps it.** "SLAM" fills the top quarter, usually red
   (sometimes black, yellow, gold or blue), and the player's head sits in front of the letters.
   Don Morris redrew it in 1996 from Neville Brody's FF Harlem.
3. **The main coverline changes face every issue.** Each cover sets its big line in a different
   display face: stencil, typewriter, script, distressed, all-lowercase, stretched. Fonts In Use
   tracks the move from "the distorted grunge era" (FF Blur, Impact, Franklin Gothic, Feb 1997)
   to House Gothic 23 and Garage Gothic caps (1998–2000).
4. **Stars and stripes as the only ornaments.** ★ between names in coverline strips, three
   stacked stars beside the logo, and stacked horizontal stripe rules above and below
   flush-right name lists.
5. **Coverline stacks in three or four colours.** Condensed caps in white, yellow, red (and
   sometimes cyan), with hard black drop shadows on the biggest line. A strap line runs across
   the very top ("The In Your Face Basketball Magazine").
6. **Real magazine furniture.** Issue date, price, barcode box, page numbers in coverlines
   ("PG. 42"). Named departments: Trash Talk (letters), In Your Face, Slam-a-da-month, Last Shot
   (a single back-page photo).
7. **Where it came from.** Creative director Don Morris came out of hardcore punk flyers and album
   art and had worked under Tibor Kalman. His approach is described as an "abrasive" template of
   aggressive imagery and bold type, with a hip-hop spin on basketball.

## Other vintage print worth borrowing from

- **Street & Smith's yearbook (1970-71):** a giant slanted "Basketball" title, with the coverlines
  as a list of contents (Over 250 Scouting Reports · Features Top Stars · Best Bets · Schedules ·
  Statistics), each underlined. Price in cents on the cover.
- **Trading card backs (Topps 70s, Fleer 1986-87, NBA Hoops 1989-91):** the original "stats on a
  small page": card number, bio line, year-by-year stat lines with a totals row, a trivia
  panel, often two inks on grey board.
- **Newspaper agate:** box scores and standings in tiny condensed type, dot leaders, bold
  leaders. The stats medium before the internet.
- **Eastbay catalogue:** dense product grids with spec callouts and model numbers.

## What was wrong with the first mock-up

It used the stock "retro zine" kit that generated designs reach for, and none of it came from
these magazines:

- tape strips, torn-paper edges, rubber stamps with a worn mask, starbursts;
- a marker "scrawl" font (Permanent Marker), a film-grain overlay, halftone dot overlays;
- everything tilted a few degrees; hard offset box shadows on every panel (the "neo-brutalist"
  web look);
- the most common free display faces (Anton, Bowlby One);
- sticker pink and neon accents that none of the covers looked at use.

## Proposed direction: "The type is the photo"

We can't use player photos, so the one thing SLAM relied on most is off the table. The fix is to
make type and data carry the weight the photography carried, and take only SLAM's real devices.

1. **Every night is an issue.** "Vol. 1, No. 1" on opening night, counting up. Each issue gets one
   flat background colour for its cover (like SLAM's yellow, red and black issues), rotating
   through a fixed set.
2. **The cover star's name is the picture.** The player's surname, set enormous, overlaps the
   BUCKETWEIGHTS wordmark the way a player's head overlapped the SLAM logo. The main coverline
   ("COLD, NOT BROKE.") rotates through three or four display faces by issue, so covers don't all
   look the same.
3. **A drawn wordmark, not a font.** BUCKETWEIGHTS as an SVG wordmark: stretched heavy grotesque
   in the spirit of the mid-90s logo, with three stacked stars. No basketball emoji-style icon.
4. **Ornaments: stars and stripes only.** ★ separators, stacked stripe rules, hard drop shadows
   on the single biggest line. Nothing tilted, taped or stamped.
5. **Inside pages are print, not cards.** Lists and tables set like newspaper agate and card
   backs. Player pages become card backs: card number, bio line, season-by-season stat lines,
   then the "at his true level" line. Verdicts become coverline labels, not stamps.
6. **Type families with real print history:** an American gothic for headlines (League Gothic, a
   revival of Alternate Gothic, the classic sports-page face); Franklin Gothic for text (Libre
   Franklin); a typewriter face for agate notes. Optional: license House Gothic 23 from House
   Industries, the face SLAM actually used around 2000.
7. **Colour from the covers:** SLAM red, yellow, black, white and cyan, plus the issue colour.
   Hot and cold stay for shooting (orange-red and blue). No pink, no neon.
8. **Departments with names:** Last Night · Heat Check / Defrost · Trash Talk (the Q&A) ·
   Last Shot (one stat, huge, at the bottom of every page).

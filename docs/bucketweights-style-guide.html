Trash talk

Letters to the editor (ok, questions we expect)

"So he's DUE?"

Nope. Nobody is owed buckets. His next three goes in at his true rate, about 38.5%, however cold he's been. The bounce-back is that rate catching up, not a make-up call.

"Where's 'true level' from?"

His last three seasons and this one, recent games counting more, pulled toward the league average until he's shot enough to trust. For threes that takes about 300 attempts; for free throws, about 25.

"Can you see how open the shots were?"

No. Public data doesn't track defenders. If a player's shots get harder (new team, bigger role) we'll call it luck when it isn't. We say so on every page.

Rules

  1. Cover loud, tables calm. Stickers, stamps, bursts and tilts are for the cover story, section heads and one thing per card. Tables and numbers stay straight, on paper, in ink.
  2. Hard shadows, square corners. 3px ink borders and a 6px offset shadow. No soft shadows, no gradients except the heat/ice list heads.
  3. Hot and cold, not green and red. Above a player's true level is hot; below is cold. Colour is never the only signal: there's always a word, sign or icon.
  4. Every projection says what it holds fixed ("at the same shots and minutes") and has a range or sample size close by.
  5. Never "due". See the vocabulary below. The voice is loud; the stats are honest.
  6. No logos, no photos. Teams are ink abbreviation badges; players are big type. The halftone, court lines and graphics carry the visuals.
  7. One spin, one scroll. The ticker and the logo ball move; both stop for reduced-motion settings. Nothing else animates.
  8. Phone first. Every layout stacks to one column at 375px with a 16px gutter; tilts drop to zero on cards when stacked.
